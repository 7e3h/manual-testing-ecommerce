**[Registration] Password Field Accepts Leading and Trailing Whitespace Without Warning, Causing Subsequent Login Failure**

## 2. Observed Behavior
During new user registration, entering a password containing leading or trailing whitespace (e.g. `"  Pass1234!  "`) is accepted without any warning message. When the user later attempts to log in typing their intended password without spaces (`"Pass1234!"`), authentication fails with *"Invalid email or password"*.

---

## 3. Preconditions
1. User is on the User Registration page (`/auth/register`).
2. Email address used is not already registered.

---

## 4. Steps to Reproduce
1. Navigate to `/auth/register`.
2. Fill all mandatory fields with valid personal details.
3. In the Password and Confirm Password fields, enter:
   `"  TestPass2026!  "` (with 2 spaces before and 2 spaces after).
4. Click "Register".
5. Observe registration success banner and redirect to `/auth/login`.
6. On the login form, enter the registered email.
7. In the password field, enter `"TestPass2026!"` (without spaces).
8. Click "Sign in".

---

## 5. Expected Result
- **Expected validation:** The registration form should either display a validation warning (e.g., *"Password cannot begin or end with whitespace"*) or automatically trim surrounding spaces prior to account creation so subsequent normal logins succeed.

---

## 6. Actual Result
- The registration form accepted the whitespace-padded password without any prompt.
- The subsequent login attempt without spaces failed with an invalid credentials error.

---

## 7. Observed Diagnostics (Browser DevTools)

### DevTools Network Tab:
- **Registration Request:** `POST /api/users/register` returned `201 Created`.
- **Subsequent Login Request:** `POST /api/users/login` without whitespace returned `401 Unauthorized` (`{ "error": "Invalid email or password" }`).

---

## 8. Possible Root Cause (Preliminary Investigation)
The password input field may not be trimmed or validated against leading/trailing whitespace before submission. If whitespace is preserved in the stored password hash, users who accidentally copy/paste spaces will experience login failures. Further review of the form validation and hashing flow is recommended.
